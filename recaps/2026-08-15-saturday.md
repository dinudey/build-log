## This week's nudge

No activity this week. Three experiments worth trying — each doable in 1–2 hours.

**1. Finish the ethics analyzer — turn wireframes into a working tool**
The hypothesis is already written. The wireframes exist. The missing piece is a working prototype: paste or link any UI artifact and get back a structured ethics audit across the five dimensions, scoped to design-level actions. Open a Claude Code session with `wireframes.html` as context and build the simplest possible interactive version — even just a form that takes a screenshot or URL and renders structured output. The question to answer by end of session: does the audit output feel actionable to you as a designer, or just descriptive? That answer decides whether the experiment is worth finishing or should be shelved.

**2. Build a "decision cost" commit hook for design projects**
Set up a Claude Code PostSave or PostCommit hook that writes a one-sentence log entry every time you save a design artifact: what changed and what it would cost to reverse it. Run it for five working days on any active project. At the end of the week, read the log and see whether the automated entries surface the things that actually mattered — the irreversible calls, the scope cuts, the structure decisions — or whether automated reflection is just noise. One hour to set up, one week of passive data collection. The output is a clear yes/no on whether AI-assisted decision logging belongs in your practice.

**3. Map your last 10 design decisions against AI leverage**
Open a blank document and list 10 decisions from your most recent design project — any size, any phase. For each one, write one sentence: at what point could Claude have changed the outcome, and how? Early framing? Critique? Spec writing? Handoff? The output is a personal heat map of where you're leaving leverage on the table. Takes 60–90 minutes, produces a reusable lens for scoping future experiments, and tells you which phase of your design process is the most underexplored territory for AI-native practice.
